How to Fix Slow Wi-Fi at Home

Slow Wi-Fi is usually caused by one of three things: router placement, channel congestion, or an overloaded device. Start by moving your router to a central, elevated location — away from walls, microwaves, and cordless phones which all interfere with 2.4 GHz signals. Log into your router settings and switch to the 5 GHz band for devices that are close by, since it's faster even though it doesn't travel as far. If you have a large home, a mesh Wi-Fi system like Google Nest or Eero is far more effective than a single router with extenders. Finally, restarting your router once a week clears cached connections and often makes a noticeable difference. If you've tried all of this and still have problems, the issue is often with your ISP or modem — call us and we'll diagnose it for free over the phone.

Smart TV Setup: What Most People Get Wrong

Setting up a Smart TV sounds straightforward, but there are several steps most people miss. First, connect your TV to the 5 GHz Wi-Fi band rather than 2.4 GHz — streaming 4K video needs the extra bandwidth. Second, go into your TV's picture settings and disable the default "Vivid" or "Dynamic" mode, which is tuned for bright showroom floors, not home viewing — "Movie" or "Filmmaker" mode is far more accurate. Third, if you're adding a soundbar, use an HDMI ARC or eARC connection rather than optical cable for the best audio quality and so the TV remote can control the volume. Finally, check whether your TV has a software update available — manufacturers regularly push fixes that improve app stability and streaming performance. If you're connecting multiple streaming devices like an Apple TV, Fire Stick, or Chromecast alongside the TV's built-in apps, we can set everything up on a single remote so switching is easy.

How to Tell If Your Computer Has a Virus

Not every slow computer has a virus, but there are specific warning signs to watch for. Sudden browser redirects to search engines you didn't choose, unexpected pop-up ads (especially ones that can't be closed), programs launching on their own, and a computer fan running constantly at full speed are all red flags. On Windows, open Task Manager (Ctrl+Shift+Esc) and look at the CPU and Memory columns — if an unfamiliar process is using over 30% of your CPU continuously, that's suspicious. Legitimate antivirus software like Malwarebytes (free version) can scan for and remove most common threats in under 10 minutes. However, some infections — particularly rootkits and browser hijackers — are harder to remove and may require a full system reset. If your computer has become unusable or you're seeing banking-related warnings, stop using it for financial tasks immediately and call us.
